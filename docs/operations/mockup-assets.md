# Mockup images and text sources

The 75 backgrounds in `public/mockups/` comprise 70 photographs and five
existing 3D mockup renders, freely licensed from Pexels, Unsplash and Pixabay.
No AI-generated backgrounds are used. Books, pamphlets, editorial spreads,
working papers, placards and public posters provide context for the political
and literary copy. A red pocket pamphlet, black cloth covers, kraft notebooks, cream
paper, newsprint, street lightboxes, a nighttime bus stop, paired wall posters,
and a freestanding event banner broaden the materials and settings. Postal
envelopes, correspondence cards, clipped prints and an open cream notebook
add smaller everyday print formats. Four phones, two laptops, a desktop monitor
and a tablet provide digital reading contexts. The urban photographs include
a night street lightbox, an airport digital display, a concrete facade billboard,
a terminal lightbox and an industrial wall poster. A cinema, a drive-in
screen and a sunlit street lightbox extend the range of illuminated and large
surfaces.
Five art photographs add a handheld magazine, a spiral sketchbook on black
timber, an empty gallery wall and two studio canvases.
Tight framing makes the paper, screen or canvas print surface the focus, with the
original materials, concrete, metal and surrounding city details retained.
The collection favours neutral paper, textiles and natural city light. The red
pocket pamphlet and blue physical billboard retain their photographed colours;
image pixels are not recoloured. Mockup lettering uses neutral ink throughout.
The text is a contemporary font specimen; these are not historical editions
or photographs of political events.

## Asset preparation

The local files preserve their source image's proportions and are converted to
WebP with `cwebp`. The six retained original assets use quality 82 and a width
of 1,800 pixels. The retained `editorial-*` and `print-*` assets use quality 80 and a width
of 1,600 pixels, except `print-08.webp`, which is 1,000 pixels wide to control its
textured background's size. The eleven `showcase-*` assets use quality 82 and
at most 1,800 pixels on the longest side, without upscaling smaller sources.
The fourteen `collection-*` assets also use quality 82 and a maximum longest
side of 1,800 pixels. The nine `icon-*` assets use quality 82, method 6,
and a maximum longest side of 1,800 pixels. The five `extra-*` photographs
also retain source proportions with a maximum longest side of 1,800 pixels.
The eight `device-*` photographs use quality 82, method 6, and a maximum longest
side of 1,800 pixels. The five `urban-night-*` and `urban-scale-*` photographs
use the same quality, method and maximum longest side. Their source photographs
already contain blank display areas; conversion only resizes and encodes them.
The three `metro-night-*` and `metro-scale-*` photographs also use
quality 82, method 6, and a maximum longest side of 1,800 pixels, preserving
their original ambient light and material texture.
The five `arts-*` photographs use quality 82, method 6, and a maximum longest
side of 1,800 pixels. Their original paper grain, canvas texture, timber and
natural light remain intact; app crops emphasize the measured print surfaces.
The conversion does not add lettering or alter the scene.

Typography remains live HTML in the selected font. Each scene has a `crop`
rectangle in stored-image pixels that defines its visible frame. The full image
and its projected typography share one translated and scaled coordinate system;
the frame clips both together. This keeps lettering registered to its photographed surface
while removing empty scenery. The stored images retain their full framing.

Four pixel coordinates for each print surface, ordered top-left,
top-right, bottom-right and bottom-left, define its CSS perspective transform. Coordinates,
logical plane dimensions and crops live in `src/data/mockup-scenes.ts`.
An optional `verso` adds a second independently projected page, allowing open
publications to show both a title and running text or chapter contents. On
curved spreads the measured surfaces stay within flatter inner page areas;
clipboard surfaces avoid clips and fingers. Screen surfaces remain inside the
active display, avoiding bezels, notches, rounded corners and home indicators.
Changing an image requires checking
its dimensions, crop, surface coordinates and any second-page geometry together.

Mockup lettering uses Flexoki neutral ink: dark `#100f0f` by default, light
`#fffcf0` on black paper and unlit signage. The photographs retain their
original colours and lighting.
Headlines preserve whole words and shrink to fit their surface after font
loading instead of splitting long words.
The selected font’s real faces remain in use throughout.

Eight device scenes use `composition: "screen"` for a digital reading layout. The
selected font appears in the navigation label, author credit, article heading,
body copy and publication footer. Portrait screens stack the heading above the
body; wide screens place them in two columns. Lucide book and search icons
frame the decorative navigation bar. Transparent overlays preserve the original
screen lighting.

The street and terminal lightboxes use poster layouts. Seven scenes use
`composition: "billboard"`: the airport display, digital billboard, concrete
billboard, industrial wall poster, cinema screen, drive-in screen and gallery wall.
Smaller author credits,
supporting copy, footers and spacing leave room for dominant headlines on
wide surfaces. Crops retain the street glow, concrete, escalator rails and
hard shadows around the measured text areas. The industrial wall crop excludes
a small parking notice, and its surface uses only the visible paper beneath
the partly cropped frame.

The cinema’s measured surface keeps the type inside the screen edges;
padding accommodates its slight curvature. The drive-in crop preserves its
corrugated surface, evening light and steel supports. The sunlit lightbox
retains the metal pedestal and street setting, with its type inset from the
frame’s rounded corners. All three retain the full source image in the local
file; only crop metadata controls the visible framing.

Four art scenes use `composition: "exhibition"`: a small author credit above
large left-aligned type, a short supporting passage where present, and a year
aligned to the lower right. The magazine’s separately projected second page
carries a short Benjamin excerpt in the clear strip above the reader’s forearm.
The sketchbook surface avoids its spiral binding and shadow; canvas surfaces
clear easel clamps and edge bevels. Gallery wall lettering uses the billboard
layout and stays within the white wall, clear of the corner and fixtures.
All five use neutral dark ink over the unchanged photographs, with no external
captions or added taglines.

The repeating canvas uses a plain, solid background and mounts only copies needed
around its viewport. It eagerly loads images so WebKit reliably displays
the transformed photo layers. Repeated copies share the same local image URL
and browser cache, so repetition does not multiply the
network assets. All 75 images remain local files, with no image service called
at runtime. `App.tsx` lazily imports
`FontDetailPage`, keeping the gallery and canvas code out of the library
view’s initial download.

The 21 scenes marked `setting: "urban"` or `setting: "art"` lead the canvas
together: 16 urban scenes plus five art scenes.
This combined group and the other 54 scenes are independently shuffled
when the gallery mounts, then stay stable while editing and exploring.
